# Sprint planning with agents (Course 804, lesson 6.5)

The sprint method here is from **Chloe Rhys's sprint planning module (703)**. The volume guidance is from the launching ad campaigns module (601). A sprint runs in three stages: intake and briefing → production and review → launch and learn.

## Inputs
Ask for what's missing. **Always ask before fixing numbers.**
- Monthly ad spend
- Team capacity for the sprint: who is available, and how many concepts or assets they can make
- The latest readout in `06-pipeline/readouts/`
- `04-concepts/concept-matrix.md` and `04-concepts/backlog.md`
- Iteration briefs in `06-pipeline/iterations/`
- Launch, promotion and stock dates

## Step 1: How much creative
Use the guidance from module 601 as a starting point, then let the data guide you.

| Monthly spend | Guidance |
|---|---|
| $0 to $1K | 1 to 2 concepts a month |
| $10K to $25K | 3 to 4 concepts a month, with 3 to 5 variations each |
| $25K to $50K | 5 to 6 concepts a month |
| $50K to $100K | 2 to 4 concepts a week, 40 to 50% iterations |
| $100K to $500K | 10 to 50% of spend goes to testing. 30 to 40% new, 60 to 70% iterations |
| $500K and up | Both big swings and constant iterations |

Check the number against capacity. If the team can't make it, say so and propose what to cut.

## Step 2: The mix of request types
Chloe Rhys's four request types (module 703) and her rough mix:

| Request type | What it is | Rough share |
|---|---|---|
| More | Iterate on a winner | 30 to 40% |
| Fix | A near-miss. Change one or two things | 20 to 30% |
| Adapt | Take a proven structure, angle or format (your own or another brand's) to a new product or persona | 10 to 20% |
| New | A net-new, untested concept | 20 to 30% |

An Adapt from another brand takes the structure only. Research to inspire, not to copy.

Adjust to the brand's stage. Propose a mix, give the reason, and **ask the strategist to confirm it**.

## Step 3: Choose the jobs
Fill the mix from the readout's candidates, the iteration briefs, the prioritized matrix rows and the backlog. One row per concept. Mark what is **time-sensitive** (a trend, a promotion with an end date). Use rolling deadlines.

## Step 4: Write the plan
Save to `06-pipeline/sprints/<YYYY-MM-DD>.md`:

1. **Goal of the sprint**, in one or two lines, tied to the North Star.
2. **Jobs:** `concept | request type | owner | turnaround | status`. An agent's job names the person who approves it.
3. **What is time-sensitive.**
4. **Win criteria** (module 703): beats the CPA goal, at scale (about 20 times the CPA in spend), across several variants, holds for 5 to 7 days or more. If `02-language/how-we-read-the-ad-account.md` sets different criteria, use the team's.
5. **Agendas for the three weekly meetings**, a few lines each:
   - **Performance review:** the readout, winners, "too early to call", what to stop.
   - **Iteration planning:** which More, Fix and Adapt jobs go in, which play for each.
   - **Concept brainstorm:** New candidates from the matrix, and Adapt ideas from research. Each person brings their own idea first.
6. **Backlog:** what didn't make it, with the reason, so nothing is lost.

Keep a single tracker: every job matches one row in `06-pipeline/creative-pipeline.md` or `04-concepts/backlog.md`.

## Step 5: Show and confirm
Show the plan and what's waiting on the strategist.

**Agents prepare. People approve.** The plan is a draft until the strategist confirms it. This skill never assigns work, messages the team or books meetings without the user's approval.

## Rules
- Never invent spend, capacity, results or deadlines.
- Every job carries its hypothesis, or points to the row that has it.
