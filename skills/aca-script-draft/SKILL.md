# Script draft (Course 804, lesson 4.2)

This follows the scriptwriting and storyboarding module (403). The agent drafts. The strategist edits, and the strategist's edit is where the script gets good.

## Before you start, read
- the row in `04-concepts/concept-matrix.md` (persona, angle, awareness level, ad type, offer, hypothesis)
- `03-research/audience/customer-voice.md` and `04-concepts/personas.md`
- `02-language/building-blocks.md` and `02-language/ad-types.md`
- `01-brand/dos-and-donts.md`, `01-brand/messaging.md`, `01-brand/offers.md`
- hooks already chosen in `05-production/hooks/<concept>.md`, if any

Ask for anything missing: the row, the video ad type, and the target length (15s, 30s, or other).

## Step 1: Choose how to write it
| Ad type | Write |
|---|---|
| Scripted types (storytelling, before and after, green screen tutorial, customer review, listicle, product explainer, founder story, VSL and similar) | The script, word for word, in building blocks |
| Organic-feeling types (comedy skit, trend, street interview) | **Direction and beats**, not word-for-word lines. Say what happens, what must be said, and what the person is free to say their own way |

## Step 2: Draft in building blocks
Lay it out as a table: `block | building block | script or direction | visual note`.
- **Hook:** three variations. Use the picked hooks, or run the `aca-hook-factory` steps. Each one must be visually different.
- **Body:** the blocks the team uses in `02-language/building-blocks.md`, in an order that fits the ad type.
- **CTA:** one, matching the campaign objective and the offer.

Use the customer's words where you can, with source IDs for verbatim lines.

## Step 3: Plain language and length
- Hemingway grade 5 or lower. Short words, short sentences, contractions.
- Count the words and give an **estimated reading time** against the target length. Say the pace you assumed. If it runs long, cut. Don't speed up.

## Step 4: Review your own draft
Answer each question in one or two plain lines. Say where the draft is weak. Don't defend it.

1. Is the persona clearly defined?
2. Are the main problems and benefits clearly shown?
3. Would a first-time viewer understand the offer?
4. Could a 10-year-old understand the language?
5. Where's the feeling?

## Step 5: Flag claims and disclaimers
List every line that makes a claim, and whether `01-brand/dos-and-donts.md` allows it. Note disclaimers the ad may need (paid actor, results may vary, AI-generated avatar, simulated imagery). Say once that you're not a lawyer and legal review is the brand's call.

## Output
Save the draft to `05-production/scripts/<concept>-draft.md` with the row number, the hypothesis, the table, the reading time, the self-review and the flags.

**Stop here. Show the draft and wait for the strategist's edit and approval.** Don't build a storyboard, a brief or any asset from an unapproved script. When it's approved, save it as `<concept>-approved.md`, note what the strategist changed in the corrections log if it should apply next time, and suggest `aca-storyboard` (studio or in-house) or `aca-creator-brief` (creators).

## Rules
- **Agents prepare. People approve.** Nothing goes to a creator, an editor or an ad account from this skill.
- Never invent testimonials, numbers, results or product facts.
- One message per ad.
- Follow the mission control file's do's and don'ts. No banned words, no out-of-stock products, no offers the brand doesn't allow.
