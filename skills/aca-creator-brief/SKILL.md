# Creative production brief (Course 804, lesson 4.3)

The scriptwriting and storyboarding module (403) gives two routes. A full storyboard suits in-house or studio work. A creative production brief suits creators. A good brief is clear enough that the creator doesn't need a call, and open enough that they still sound like themselves.

## Inputs
- The concept: one row of `04-concepts/concept-matrix.md`, plus the approved script or talking points if they exist.
- The creator, from `05-production/creators/tracker.csv`, and what they're good at.
- `04-concepts/personas.md`, `03-research/audience/customer-voice.md`.
- The mission control file and `01-brand/dos-and-donts.md`.

If the row, the creator or the deliverables are missing, ask.

## The brief (one page)

| # | Section | What goes in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core concept and strategic objective | The idea in one line, and the hypothesis we're testing. What the viewer should feel or believe after watching |
| 2 | Target persona and emotional drivers | Who this is for and why they care, from the micro persona sheet. Two or three verbatim customer lines |
| 3 | Key message points | Three to five talking points. Points, not lines to read out |
| 4 | Hook suggestions | **At least three.** Each with the spoken line, the visual and the sound. **At least one verbatim from customer voice, with its source ID.** Ask the creator to film all of them |
| 5 | Brand guidelines and references | Branded or native, the look, and reference links with one line on what to take from each. Structure only |
| 6 | Flexible and fixed | See below |
| 7 | Deliverables, specs, usage rights | See below |
| 8 | Must say / never say | Taken from the mission control file. Point to it. Don't rewrite the rules |

### Flexible and fixed
| Fixed | Flexible |
|---|---|
| Product facts and names | Their own words and delivery |
| Must say and never say | Setting, outfit, time of day |
| The offer and CTA | Order of talking points, if the story still works |
| Required shots and disclaimers | Extra B-roll they think of |

Adjust the table to the concept. Say plainly what is fixed, so the creator isn't left wondering.

### Deliverables, specs and usage rights
- Number of videos, hook variations and raw files.
- Aspect ratio, length, sound on, lighting, whether we add supers.
- Due date, fee or gifting.
- Usage rights: where the content can run, for how long, whitelisting, and rights for image and voice. Use only the terms the user gives you. If they haven't decided, leave the line blank and list it as waiting on them.

## Rules
- Plain language, Hemingway grade 5 or lower. Creators skim.
- Talking points keep the creator sounding like themselves.
- Every stat or quote in the brief is true and sourced.
- Don't describe the creator by personal attributes. Describe the situation and the content.
- Don't promise fees, terms or rights the user hasn't confirmed.

## Output
Save to `05-production/briefs/<creator>-<concept>.md` (and to Notion or Drive if connected and asked).

**Agents prepare. People approve.** Show the brief to the user. Don't send it to the creator. A person approves and sends it.

End with what's waiting on the user: open terms, missing references, approval.

When footage comes back, `aca-creative-qa` checks it against this brief.
