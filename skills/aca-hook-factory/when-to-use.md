# When to use each play

Choose by three things: the awareness level on the matrix row, the persona, and the proof you really have. If the team keeps its own guide in `02-language/hook-categories.md`, that guide wins.

| If you see... | Choose | Don't choose it when... |
|---|---|---|
| Problem aware persona with a sharp, specific pain in customer voice | `problem-or-pain-point` | It would shame the viewer |
| A doubt or belief the persona holds, or a question they ask in comments | `question` | The answer is a claim the brand can't make |
| Unaware or problem aware persona who wants to learn, and you have several reasons or steps | `list-or-how-to` | You only have one point to make |
| Product aware or most aware persona, or a product that is visually striking | `product-first` | The persona doesn't know the problem yet |
| Solution aware persona, and you have a real outcome to show | `result-or-benefit` | There is no evidence for the result |
| A persona with a clear situation or identity (a nurse on a long shift) | `call-out` | The persona is vague |
| A vivid first-person review line, a liked comment, or review volume you can prove | `experience-and-proof` | The line is a health or performance claim |
| The situation is light and relatable, and the brand voice allows humor | `funny` | The pain is sensitive |
| The persona is buying the person they want to be | `aspirational` | It would look polished in an account where polished doesn't spend |

## Rules for choosing
- For a new concept, pick three different plays so the three hooks are truly different.
- For a winner, use at least four plays across the ten hooks.
- If two plays fit, prefer the one with real proof behind it.
- If none fit, stop and ask. Don't invent a new play.
