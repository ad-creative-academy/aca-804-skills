# The iteration playbook (Course 804, lesson 6.4)

This comes from the growth creative operating system module (704). The rule of the playbook: bigger visual swings, not small tweaks. Small tweaks read as the same ad to the platform. That is the similarity penalty.

## Before you start, read
- `when-to-use.md` (always)
- the latest readout in `06-pipeline/readouts/` and the ad's row in `06-pipeline/creative-pipeline.md`
- the ad itself: script, storyboard or file, and its matrix row
- what footage, talent and assets exist in `05-production/`
- `02-language/how-we-read-the-ad-account.md` and `01-brand/dos-and-donts.md`

## Steps
1. **Confirm the ad and its status.** Is it a winner, a fatiguing winner, or a near-miss under the team's rules? If it's "too early to call", say so and stop. Don't iterate on an ad without a result.
2. **Name what works and what doesn't**, using the readout's symptom and the four tags (visual, persona, messaging, hook).
3. **Choose the plays** with `when-to-use.md`. Start your answer by saying which plays you chose and why.
4. **Follow each chosen play** in `plays/`.
5. **Write one short brief per iteration** (below).
6. **Run the similarity check** on every proposed iteration.

## The plays
| Play | In one line |
|---|---|
| `new-hooks` | 8 to 10 new hooks. Change the hook to change the audience |
| `fresh-b-roll` | New visuals over the same audio |
| `talent-refresh` | Same script, different people |
| `format-variation` | Same concept, a different format or ad type |
| `modular-remixing` | Hook of A + body of B + CTA of C |
| `bigger-visual-swing` | New scene, location or setup |

Talent refresh, format variation and modular remixing are the three-part winner playbook.

## The iteration brief
Keep each to a few lines:
- **Source ad** and why it earned an iteration (number, spend, window)
- **Play**
- **What changes** and **what stays fixed**
- **Hypothesis:** what am I testing, what do I expect to learn, how will this inform the next iteration
- **What it needs:** existing footage, a new shoot, talent, an editor's time
- **Request type** for the sprint plan, using Chloe Rhys's four types from the sprint planning module (703):

| Request type | What it is |
|---|---|
| More | Iterate on a winner |
| Fix | A near-miss. Change one or two things |
| Adapt | Take a proven structure, angle or format (your own or another brand's) to a new product or persona |
| New | A net-new, untested concept |

Most briefs from this playbook are More (a winner) or Fix (a near-miss). When a play takes a proven structure, angle or format to a new product or persona, mark it Adapt. A net-new concept doesn't belong here. It goes to `aca-concept-matrix`.

## Similarity check
For each iteration, ask: would the platform see this and the original as the same ad? **Warn clearly when it would.** A new super, a trimmed second or a color change is too similar. Suggest the bigger swing.

## Output
Save to `06-pipeline/iterations/<ad-or-concept>-<YYYY-MM-DD>.md`. Add the iterations to `04-concepts/backlog.md` as candidates. In chat, show a table: `# | play | what changes | hypothesis | cost to make | too similar?`.

**Agents prepare. People approve.** The strategist picks which iterations go into the sprint. Nothing is sent to editors, creators or the media buyer from this skill.

Suggest `aca-hook-factory` for the new-hooks play, `aca-casting` for talent refresh and `aca-sprint-plan` to schedule the work.

## Rules
- Never invent results. Every "winner" claim carries its number, spend and window.
- If no play fits, ask. Don't invent a new play.
- Follow the do's and don'ts.
