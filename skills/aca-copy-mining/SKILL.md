# Copy mining and micro personas (Course 804, lesson 3.2)

Audience research is the second pillar in the research module (302). Copy mining builds the customer empathy muscle. The goal is the customer's exact words, kept with their source, so the angles and personas that follow are built on evidence.

## Sources
- Reddit, GigaBrain, 3 and 4 star Amazon reviews, YouTube comments on competitor ads, Facebook groups, support transcripts.
- The brand's own review export and ad comments.
- **Competitor reviews** are allowed only as labeled category insight. Tag every line `competitor: <brand>` and keep it in its own section. It is never a testimonial for this brand.

Read `04-concepts/personas.md` and `01-brand/dos-and-donts.md` first if they exist. Say which sources you had and which you didn't.

Pay extra attention to 3 and 4 star reviews, comments with lots of likes, and long reviews. That's where people explain themselves.

## Step 1: Collect verbatim quotes, with source
Keep every line **word for word**. For each quote, note:

`quote | source ID (for example R014, or AD-05 comment) | where it came from | star rating or like count | persona, if it's clear`

Don't tidy the language. Don't merge two quotes into one.

## Step 2: Sort by underlying emotion, then name the angle
Read the quotes again and ask of each one: what is the feeling underneath? Group them by that emotion (for example relief, embarrassment, fear, pride, frustration, surprise). Use the emotions you find, not a fixed list.

Each emotion group points to an **angle**. Name the angle in plain words and list the quotes that support it.

| Emotion | Angle, in plain words | Supporting quotes (source IDs) |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

An angle with one quote behind it is a **candidate**. Say so.

## Step 3: Micro persona sheets
For each persona with evidence, fill one sheet:

| Field | Filled from |
|---|---|
| Who they are | Their job, situation or life stage, in their words, with source IDs |
| What they care about | The emotion groups they show up in |
| Why they buy | The moment or reason they give for buying |
| The words they use | 3 to 5 verbatim quotes |

Then add one line on the creator type to cast for this persona. Cast to persona: describe the situation and the kind of content, never personal attributes.

A persona with thin evidence is labeled **candidate**.

## Step 4: Objections, and the proof they ask for
List the doubts people voice before buying, in their words. Next to each, write the proof they ask for or the proof that changed their mind, also in their words.

| Objection (verbatim, source ID) | The proof they ask for |
|---|---|
| | |

Leave the proof cell blank if nobody says it. Don't fill it in yourself.

## Step 5: The burner account (a job for the human)
Suggest the burner account exercise from the research module: the strategist behaves like the persona for 48 hours on a fresh account and watches the feed become their feed. Offer to file their notes.

## Compliance flags
Flag any quote that sounds like a health, medical or performance claim ("lasts 48 hours"). It can inspire, but it **must not become a brand claim** without legal review. Say "I'm not a lawyer, so check with yours" once.

## Output
1. Update `03-research/audience/customer-voice.md`: the quotes by emotion with their angles, the objections and proof table, the flags, the date and the source files.
2. Update `04-concepts/personas.md` with the micro persona sheets.
3. In chat, show the three strongest angles with their quotes, five **proof lines** (short verbatim quotes strong enough to carry a static or open a video), and one new persona or angle worth testing, with its evidence.
4. Check `06-pipeline/creative-pipeline.md` and say which angles are untested.

## Never
- Rewrite customer language into brand language here. That happens at the copy step.
- Invent reviews, or merge two reviews into one quote.
- Include customer names, emails or order numbers.
